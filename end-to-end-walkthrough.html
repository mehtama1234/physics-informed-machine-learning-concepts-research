End-To-End Course Walkthrough

One Scientific Job From Start To Finish

Imagine a team has sparse measurements from a physical system, a few old simulations, a shaped domain, and a real decision to make. They need a field, property, risk, design answer, or readable rule for a new case. The course route is the plain path from that shortage to a claim that can be tested.

The route below is not a ranking of topics. It is a way to read the course when the reader wants the big picture first. Each row says the everyday need, the first topic that helps, what that topic adds, and what changed case can reject the claim.

1 Scientific Machine Learning predictions must obey the physical laws that define the problem, not just fit the data combine learned prediction with scientific checks that name what the claim is allowed to mean state the scientific quantity first, then test it under a changed case that matters in that domain
2 Partial Differential Equations a quantity changes over space and time, so one number is not enough to describe what's happening write a local change rule that uses rates across space and time change the boundary, grid, source term, or physical scale and check conservation, stability, and measured error
3 Physics-Informed Neural Networks sparse measurements from experiments, but the answer must obey known physical laws like gravity or heat flow fit a neural network while also measuring how badly its output violates the known equation move the training points, inspect sharp regions, and compare against a numerical solve or held-out measurements
4 Operator Learning engineers need to predict all the fields (temperature, pressure, flow) across a space and time for many input cases, not just one learn the map from problem input to solution, not only one solution at a time change resolution, coefficients, boundary conditions, or forcing and check whether the predicted field still satisfies the scientific quantity being claimed
5 Surrogate Modeling running a full simulation is too expensive to do for every design choice or question train a cheaper stand-in for the expensive input-output behavior compare against the full solver on new cases near the edge of the intended use
6 Graphs And Geometric Learning molecules, proteins, and meshes have a shape and connections that rows of numbers throw away let information move along the object connections instead of flattening the object into a plain row change the mesh, rotate or move the object, or add missing interactions and inspect what breaks
7 Attention For Scientific Fields a local spot in a field may depend on faraway information, but scanning everywhere costs too much let the model choose which parts of the field exchange information change the window size, inspect long-range effects, and compare behavior near boundaries or sharp structures
8 Uncertainty And Generalization a prediction from a model is only useful if you know under which conditions you should actually trust it separate fit on familiar examples from evidence on changed examples move one important condition outside the training range and measure the first failure
9 Optimization For Learning models need a way to measure progress so they know which settings are better or worse change model settings to lower the written score inspect what the score ignores, then check whether the ignored behavior fails after training
10 Generative Modeling some tasks need many plausible examples, not a single best prediction, to explore what's possible learn how to sample new candidates that resemble the training family measure constraints, rare cases, conservation, and downstream task performance on generated samples
11 Neural Differential Equations scientists watch a system change over time but don't know the rule driving that change learn the missing rate rule and place it inside a time-evolution calculation run longer than the training window and check whether small rate errors accumulate into drift
12 Symbolic Regression And Model Discovery a scientist may need a readable equation, not a numerical model that predicts but cannot be understood search for a readable equation that fits the data and survives a changed case remove a needed variable, add noise, or test a new experiment and see whether the formula still predicts
13 Foundation Models For PDEs scientists train separate models for each PDE, each grid size, each parameter—expensive duplication train one broad model to reuse structure across many related field-prediction tasks hold out a new equation family, boundary type, scale, or rare regime and compare against a trusted solver
14 Deep Learning scientists have many measured examples but no formula that predicts the next case adjust many weights until the model maps familiar inputs to the right outputs hold out a changed material, geometry, parameter range, or sensor condition

Shape And Field Checks

Any route through the course should pause when the scientific object has a shape, boundary, mesh, molecule, graph, surface, or field. Ask what relation carries the quantity. Then change that relation: move a boundary, refine a mesh, add a missing link, hold out a molecule family, shift a field region, or test a rare flow case.

What The Final Claim Can Say

The final claim should be modest and useful. It can say which evidence was used, which answer was needed, which mathematical move carried the answer, which field decision used it, and which changed case was checked. It should not claim that the method works everywhere, or that the course label itself proves anything.

Final Say-It-Back Test

A reader understands the whole course when they can tell the story without hiding behind a method name: here is the real scientific job, here is the evidence, here is the hidden answer, here is the topic route, here is where shape or topology enters, here is the field where the answer matters, and here is the changed case that can reject the claim.
