Domain Map: Where The Ideas Show Up

This page starts from application areas instead of method names. The same course ideas repeat across domains: make expensive prediction cheaper, infer hidden state from partial evidence, preserve geometry or topology, choose actions safely, and attach uncertainty to decisions.

Fluids And Turbulence

Problem: flows have many coupled quantities across space and time. Vortices move, shocks form, turbulence becomes chaotic, and full solvers can be expensive.

Why learning enters: to build faster surrogates, recover missing flow data, find coherent structures, learn operators, and reduce dynamics without losing transition boundaries.

What to protect: conservation, stability, moving structures, turbulence statistics, and regime boundaries such as laminar versus turbulent futures.

Read: Chapter 3, Chapter 5, Chapter 113, Chapter 136.

Structures And Solid Mechanics

Problem: structures can bend, buckle, crack, vibrate, and couple small local damage to large global motion.

Why learning enters: to reduce large finite-element models, handle local nonlinear behavior, infer damage, and explore many design or loading cases.

What to protect: force balance, energy behavior, instability thresholds, contact or crack location, and the difference between local accuracy and global safety.

Read: Chapter 4, Chapter 10, Chapter 91, Chapter 136.

Medicine And Biology

Problem: living systems combine mechanics, flow, electricity, chemistry, cells, tissue, patient variation, and noisy measurements.

Why learning enters: to personalize models, fuse sparse clinical data with physics, infer hidden states, and make simulations usable for decision support.

What to protect: patient-specific meaning, uncertainty, causal limits, biological constraints, and the difference between correlation and actionable mechanism.

Read: Chapter 6, Chapter 87, Chapter 95, Chapter 134.

Materials And Chemistry

Problem: material behavior depends on atoms, molecules, microstructure, defects, local environments, and processing history.

Why learning enters: to make expensive quantum or molecular calculations cheaper, learn local energy models, transfer across materials, and connect microstructure to large-scale properties.

What to protect: symmetries, forces, energy consistency, chemical validity, local environment structure, and out-of-distribution checks.

Read: Chapter 21, Chapter 122, Chapter 127, Chapter 132.

Plasma And Particle Systems

Problem: particle simulations can be noisy and expensive, while plasma physics must preserve structure over long time scales.

Why learning enters: to reduce particle counts, improve particle representations, add collisions efficiently, and keep coarse simulations faithful to the physics.

What to protect: Hamiltonian structure, moments, entropy, energy correction, collision behavior, and long-time stability.

Read: Chapter 126, Chapter 130, Chapter 133.

Climate, Ocean, And Weather

Problem: earth systems are large, chaotic, partially observed, and expensive to simulate at high resolution.

Why learning enters: to reduce model cost, assimilate observations, bridge model hierarchies, forecast under uncertainty, and build digital-twin loops.

What to protect: conserved quantities, multiscale coupling, uncertainty growth, causal interpretation, and honest limits on predictability.

Read: Chapter 121, Chapter 134, Chapter 135, Chapter 136.

Manufacturing, Design, And Topology

Problem: design changes geometry, and geometry changes the physical field. In manufacturing, the process path can determine the final material state.

Why learning enters: to accelerate design search, differentiate through physics, connect process controls to material outcomes, and guide topology or geometry optimization.

What to protect: constraints, connected pathways, interfaces, process history, safety limits, and changed-case validation under new shapes.

Read: Chapter 58, Chapter 69, Chapter 128, Chapter 129.

Subsurface Flow And Energy Systems

Problem: the underground is mostly hidden. Wells are sparse and expensive, while decisions about geothermal energy, groundwater, carbon storage, and fault risk still have to be made.

Why learning enters: to generate plausible geological worlds, assimilate sparse observations, forecast future CO2 or heat movement, and choose monitoring plans.

What to protect: fracture connectivity, pressure and stress coupling, prior bias checks, physical validity of generated samples, and uncertainty in future decisions.

Read: Chapter 134, Chapter 135, Chapter 137.

The Cross-Domain Lesson

The same question works everywhere: what is the physical thing that must not be lost when learning makes the problem faster or more convenient? In fluids it may be a moving vortex or transition boundary. In topology design it may be a connected path. In materials it may be force and symmetry. In subsurface flow it may be fracture connectivity and uncertainty. The method name changes, but the burden remains concrete.
