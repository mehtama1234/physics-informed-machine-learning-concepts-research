PINNs From Zero

A PINN is trying to answer a very practical question: can a learning model fill in a physical answer when we have only some measurements and some rules?

Before The Name

Imagine you have only a few dots of evidence, but the thing you need is a whole map. You do not want a model that merely draws a smooth picture through the dots. You want a model whose whole picture still behaves like the physical thing.

few dotssensor readings, lab measurements, or simulation samples
known ruleheat spreads, mass is conserved, waves move, boundaries matter
whole answertemperature, pressure, velocity, stress, concentration
trust testdoes the guessed map obey the rule away from the dots?

The method name is less important than this burden: use sparse evidence without letting the model invent a physically impossible world between the evidence points.

The Simple Story

You have a metal plate. You know the temperature at a few spots. You also know heat should spread smoothly from hot places toward cold places. You want the temperature everywhere, not just at the measured spots.

A normal learning model can learn from the measured spots. A PINN also asks whether the guessed temperature map behaves like heat should behave.

The dots are measurements. The bands are the kind of whole-field pattern we are trying to infer. The physical rule helps fill the space between the dots.

What Problem Is Being Solved?

The problem is not just missing data. Missing data alone could be handled by many fitting tools. The harder problem is missing data plus a physical rule that should still hold in places where no sensor was placed.

That is why PINNs matter in heat flow, fluid flow, materials, biology, and many engineering settings. In all of them, a few measurements may be available, but the decision depends on the whole field: where heat peaks, where pressure drops, where stress concentrates, or where a concentration front moves next.

The Three Teachers

A PINN is trained by listening to several teachers at the same time.

Data teacher Says: match the measured points we actually observed.
Physics teacher Says: your full guessed field should obey the equation.
Boundary teacher Says: respect what is known at the edges or at the start.

This is the core idea. A PINN does not only fit data. It is judged by data, physical rules, and boundary information together.

Why This Can Fail

The failure is also simple: the teachers may not be heard equally.

If the data teacher shouts and the physics teacher whispers, the model may fit the measured points while breaking the physical rule. If the physics teacher shouts and the data teacher whispers, the model may obey a clean-looking equation but miss the real observations. If the boundary teacher is ignored, the answer may be wrong at the edges even if the middle looks plausible.

Imbalanced

The loud term drives the model. The quiet term may be ignored.

Balanced Enough

Each important demand affects the answer.

What The DDPS Talk Adds

The talk is not saying PINNs are useless. It is saying the clean story hides a training problem.

The first-principles issue is that a PINN is not solved when you write down the loss. It is solved only if training can actually find a field that satisfies the important pieces at the same time.

Bad Claim

"We added the equation to the loss, so the answer is physical."

Better Claim

"We checked whether data error, equation error, and boundary error all mattered during training."

How To Understand The Word Residual

Residual just means leftover error.

If the equation says a correct temperature field should behave a certain way, and your guessed field does not behave that way, the difference is the residual. Small residual means the guess is closer to obeying the equation. Large residual means the guess is breaking the rule.

How To Understand The Word Loss

Loss is the score the model tries to lower.

A PINN loss is usually a combined score: data mistake plus equation mistake plus boundary mistake. Training means adjusting the network to lower that combined score.

Accept Or Reject The PINN Claim

Accept only if data, equation, and boundary demands all changed the learned answer.
Reject if the paper only says the equation was added, without showing that training listened to it.
Ask next what happens when sensors move, boundaries change, noise increases, or the regime shifts?

The One Sentence To Keep

A PINN is a learned physical field, but it is only trustworthy if training made the data, equation, and boundary demands all count.
