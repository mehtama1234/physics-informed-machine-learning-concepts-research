Quick Chooser

Everyday Problem Study This Family Why It Helps First Thing To Check Where To Read
You have few measurements, but you know the equation should be obeyed. Physics-informed learning It uses the equation as training pressure when data is sparse. Do the loss terms balance, or does one rule drown out the others? PINNs From Zero, Chapter 1, Chapter 102
The full simulator is too slow to run thousands of times. Reduced order modeling It builds a smaller stand-in for repeated prediction, design, or control. Does the small model preserve the motion, not just the snapshots? ROMs From Zero, Chapter 9, Chapter 91
The important feature moves, bends, shocks, or changes regime. Transport-aware and nonlinear coordinates They stop a simple moving pattern from looking artificially high-dimensional. Can the representation follow the moving feature in a changed case? Chapter 18, Chapter 24, Chapter 47
You need a rule from one whole field to another whole field. Operator learning It learns maps between functions, such as input fields to solution fields. Does it survive new grids, boundary conditions, geometries, or regimes? Chapter 98, Chapter 125, Chapter 131
You must choose sensors or reconstruct a full state from sparse data. State estimation and sensor placement They ask which measurements reveal the hidden state well enough. Are the chosen sensors actually seeing the modes or features that matter? Chapter 13, Chapter 80, Chapter 123
The model must decide an action, not only predict a field. Control and differentiable simulation They connect prediction to gradients, policies, constraints, and decisions. Does the controller exploit a model blind spot or violate safety? Chapter 8, Chapter 58, Chapter 128
The shape or connectivity of the design changes the physics. Geometry-aware and topology-aware learning They keep track of how physical shape changes fields, regimes, and constraints. Does the model preserve the connected pathway or boundary that controls the outcome? Chapter 69, Chapter 129, Chapter 137
You want to infer the hidden equation or structure from data. Equation discovery and coherent-structure analysis They search for the simpler rule, mode, or pattern behind noisy observations. Does the discovered structure predict new time, new conditions, or only replay the data? Chapter 113, Chapter 124, Chapter 136
The system is hidden and observations arrive over time. Data assimilation It updates a model or forecast as new evidence arrives. Could the data identify the hidden state, or is the posterior just copying the prior? Chapter 134, Chapter 137
The model answer needs a believable error bar or risk estimate. Uncertainty quantification It attaches doubt to predictions, parameters, architectures, data, or discretization. Which source of uncertainty is missing from the estimate? Chapter 135, Chapter 137
You need many plausible worlds, not one best answer. Generative modeling It samples possible fields, structures, or futures from a learned distribution. Are the samples physically valid, connected correctly, and useful for the decision? Chapter 125, Chapter 131, Chapter 137
A model claims it can help across many scientific tasks. Scientific foundation models They try to reuse large learned representations across equations, materials, and geometries. Was generalization tested across real physical changes, or only nearby examples? Chapter 132, Chapter 135
