Math Map Without Formulas

This page explains the recurring math objects in the course without symbols. Use it when a chapter starts using a term like loss, residual, operator, posterior, manifold, latent space, or gradient and you need the everyday meaning first.

math word
ordinary meaning
physical job
failure check

Loss

Plain meaning: a score for how badly the model is doing.

Physical job: combine different complaints: data mismatch, equation violation, boundary error, safety violation, or regularization.

Failure check: one complaint can drown out the others, so training looks successful while the physical rule is ignored.

Read: Chapter 1, PINNs From Zero.

Residual

Plain meaning: the leftover error after you plug a proposed answer into a rule.

Physical job: tell whether the learned field actually obeys the equation, not just whether it matches stored data.

Failure check: a small residual at sampled points can miss bad behavior between points or under changed conditions.

Read: Chapter 1, Chapter 102.

Gradient

Plain meaning: a direction that says which change would increase or decrease something.

Physical job: guide training, control, inverse problems, and design by saying how a prediction changes when an input changes.

Failure check: gradients can be wrong or misleading near discontinuities, missing physics, unstable rollouts, or model blind spots.

Read: Chapter 8, Chapter 58, Chapter 129.

Basis

Plain meaning: a small set of building-block shapes used to rebuild a larger field.

Physical job: make a huge simulation cheaper by describing it with a few coefficients instead of every grid value.

Failure check: a fixed basis can waste many shapes tracking a feature that simply moved.

Read: ROMs From Zero, Chapter 9, Chapter 18.

Manifold

Plain meaning: a smaller curved place where the important states live inside a huge state space.

Physical job: preserve nonlinear motion, regime boundaries, cycles, and attractors that a flat compression can miss.

Failure check: the manifold may be only local, badly measured, not attracting, or broken by a poor coordinate chart.

Read: Manifolds From Zero, Chapter 47, Chapter 136.

Operator

Plain meaning: a rule that turns one whole object into another whole object.

Physical job: map an initial field, geometry, source, or material field to a solution field.

Failure check: the rule may fail when grid, geometry, boundary condition, resolution, or physical regime changes.

Read: Chapter 98, Chapter 125, Chapter 131.

Posterior

Plain meaning: the updated set of beliefs after observations are used.

Physical job: narrow many possible hidden worlds into the ones still consistent with data.

Failure check: if the prior excludes the true world, the posterior can become confidently wrong.

Read: Chapter 134, Chapter 137.

Latent Space

Plain meaning: a hidden compressed language used by the model.

Physical job: represent complicated fields, geometries, molecules, or fracture networks with fewer variables.

Failure check: the compressed language may hide the physical feature that matters, such as a connection, symmetry, or rare regime.

Read: Chapter 125, Chapter 129, Chapter 137.

Invariant

Plain meaning: something that stays true while the system changes.

Physical job: protect conservation laws, symmetries, stable structures, or closed reduced dynamics.

Failure check: if the learned model breaks the invariant, it may drift, create impossible states, or lose long-time trust.

Read: Chapter 126, Chapter 130, Chapter 136.

The Rule For Reading Math In This Course

Before trying to parse symbols, ask what job the math object does. Does it measure error, move information, compress a field, update belief, preserve a rule, or guide a decision? Once that job is clear, the formula has somewhere to land.
