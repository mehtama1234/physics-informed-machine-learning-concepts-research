What to know before what

Prerequisite Map

This page shows what to understand before reading each major idea. It is not a formal math prerequisite list. It is a plain dependency map: if a topic feels impossible, go back to the earlier burden it depends on.

Before PINNs

Know first: a field is a quantity spread over space or time, an equation is a rule the field should obey, and a loss is a score for what the model is violating.

Then read: From Zero, Math Map, PINNs From Zero, Chapter 1.

Ready when: you can say why matching data points is weaker than matching data plus the physical rule.

Before ROMs

Know first: repeated solves create cost, a basis is a small set of building-block shapes, and compression can lose motion.

Then read: ROMs From Zero, Chapter 9, Chapter 18.

Ready when: you can explain why a moving pulse can be simple physically but hard for a fixed basis.

Before Manifolds And Geometry

Know first: a system state can be treated as a point in a huge space, and long-term motion may live on a smaller curved shape.

Then read: Manifolds From Zero, Chapter 47, Chapter 136.

Ready when: you can say why a boundary between two futures matters more than average prediction error.

Before Operator Learning

Know first: some physics tasks map one whole field to another whole field, not one number to one number.

Then read: Math Map, Chapter 98, Chapter 125, Chapter 131.

Ready when: you can describe what changes when grid, geometry, boundary condition, or resolution changes.

Before Control And Design

Know first: prediction becomes harder when it is used to choose an action, because the action can push the model into unfamiliar cases.

Then read: Chapter 8, Chapter 58, Chapter 69, Chapter 129.

Ready when: you can explain why a design optimizer needs changed-case tests, not only old test error.

Before Data Assimilation And UQ

Know first: models and observations are both incomplete, a posterior is an updated belief, and uncertainty must match the decision.

Then read: Chapter 134, Chapter 135, Chapter 137.

Ready when: you can say why a narrow posterior can still be wrong if the prior was biased.

Before Foundation Models And Generative Models

Know first: broad reuse needs broad evidence, and plausible samples need physical validity checks.

Then read: Chapter 125, Chapter 132, Chapter 135, Chapter 137.

Ready when: you can reject a foundation or generative claim that lacks changed-domain tests.

The Rule

If a chapter feels too hard, do not jump to more advanced chapters. Go one dependency backward: physical problem, evidence, math word, method family, trust boundary. The missing piece is usually one level earlier than the confusing sentence.
