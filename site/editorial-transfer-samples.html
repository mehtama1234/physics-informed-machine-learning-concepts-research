The Transfer Test

A concept is strong only if it survives a change of scientific setting. The learner should be able to move from a fluid flow to a heated wall, a battery material, a protein system, or a climate field without changing the meaning of the concept. The words may change, but the burden stays the same: name the quantity, name the evidence, name the rule, name the approximation, and name the case that would reject the answer.

Partial Differential Equations

Ordinary pressure: a field changes from place to place and time to time, but not arbitrarily.

Hidden object: the local rule that says how nearby values constrain each other.

Invariant: nearby values must satisfy the same balance law at every point where the equation is supposed to hold.

What changes by domain: fluids conserve mass and momentum, heat follows temperature flow, materials balance load and deformation, and chemistry tracks species and reaction rates.

Failure test: predict a field that fits sample points but violates the rule between them.

Physics-Informed Neural Networks

Ordinary pressure: measurements are sparse, but the governing rule is partly known.

Hidden object: a training score that punishes both data error and rule violation.

Invariant: the model must answer to two judges at once: the observed data and the equation residual.

What changes by domain: the residual may measure flow conservation, heat balance, stress equilibrium, reaction kinetics, or a climate coupling constraint.

Failure test: improve data fit while making the residual physically impossible.

Review derivation

Operator Learning

Ordinary pressure: scientists need a reusable map from one whole field to another.

Hidden object: the transformation between functions, not a single output number.

Invariant: the learned object is a rule from input function to output function, so it must keep meaning when the grid or forcing changes.

What changes by domain: the input can be an initial flow field, a boundary temperature, a material layout, a molecular state, or a regional climate condition.

Failure test: train on one grid or boundary family and fail when the field shape changes.

Review derivation

Surrogate Modeling

Ordinary pressure: a trusted simulator is too slow to run for every design or control choice.

Hidden object: a cheaper stand-in with a named domain where it remains faithful.

Invariant: speed is useful only inside the region where the cheap model preserves the simulator's decision ranking or physical answer.

What changes by domain: the valid region may be a Reynolds-number range, a temperature window, a tested material family, a molecular class, or a design space.

Failure test: use it outside that domain and hide the error behind average accuracy.

Symbolic Regression

Ordinary pressure: a fitted predictor may work but not reveal the law a scientist can inspect.

Hidden object: a readable expression that trades accuracy against simplicity.

Invariant: the expression must stay checkable against units, limits, signs, and known behavior, not just against sample error.

What changes by domain: the allowable expression changes with conservation terms, constitutive laws, kinetic forms, scaling laws, or empirical climate relations.

Failure test: discover a formula that matches data but breaks units, limits, or known behavior.

Uncertainty And Generalization

Ordinary pressure: a scientific answer must say when it should not be trusted.

Hidden object: the distance between the new case and the evidence that trained the method.

Invariant: confidence must be tied to evidence coverage, regime shift, and physical plausibility.

What changes by domain: the warning signal may come from geometry, boundary condition, material regime, molecular environment, forcing pattern, or decision loop.

Failure test: report confidence on a case whose geometry, boundary, scale, or regime has changed.

Cross-Domain Transfer Matrix

Fluids And Flow

Quantity: velocity, pressure, density, vorticity, or transported concentration.

Known rule: local conservation and boundary behavior.

Failure: a learned field looks smooth but creates or destroys mass between measurements.

Open domain

Heat And Diffusion

Quantity: temperature, concentration, or another field that spreads from high to low.

Known rule: nearby values constrain the rate and direction of change.

Failure: the model fits sensors while violating boundary heat flow or time direction.

Open domain

Materials And Mechanics

Quantity: stress, strain, stiffness, fracture risk, or microstructure property.

Known rule: shape and load must agree with material response.

Failure: a surrogate predicts a strong design outside the tested material regime.

Open domain

Chemistry And Biology

Quantity: concentration, binding, reaction rate, molecular state, or biological response.

Known rule: conservation, kinetics, geometry, or mechanistic constraints.

Failure: a fitted rule works on known cases but breaks when scale, species, or environment changes.

Open domain

Climate And Earth Fields

Quantity: temperature, humidity, pressure, flow, radiation, or regional risk.

Known rule: coupled fields move through space and time with physical limits.

Failure: the model interpolates well locally but loses far-field coupling or extreme-event behavior.

Open domain

Design And Control Loops

Quantity: the next design choice, experiment, control input, or candidate field.

Known rule: a cheap learned model must stay faithful where decisions depend on it.

Failure: optimization exploits surrogate error and selects a design the real simulator or experiment rejects.

Concept-To-Domain Checks

PDE

If the page cannot say what must be conserved, balanced, diffused, transported, or bounded in each domain, it has taught symbols instead of a field rule.

PINN

If the page says "add physics to the loss" but does not name the residual and the data term separately, it hides the tradeoff the learner needs.

Operator

If the page only predicts one output, it has missed the object. Operator learning is about a reusable map from one whole input function to another whole output function.

Surrogate

If the page does not name the valid region of the cheap substitute, it invites the learner to trust it exactly where it is most likely to fail.

Symbolic Regression

If the discovered expression cannot be checked against units, limits, and known behavior, readability has replaced science instead of serving it.

Uncertainty

If the page reports confidence without saying what changed from the training evidence, the confidence is not a scientific warning signal.

Invariant-Domain-Failure Rule
