Common Question

What should the training process reward so the learned answer serves the scientific job?

Walk The Domain From Scratch

Begin with the quantity, not the algorithm. In this domain the quantity is the scientific quantity encoded by the terms of the training score. A useful page has to say where that quantity lives, how it is observed, and why a wrong estimate would matter. The hard part is not that the domain has technical vocabulary. The hard part is that the model reduces the written score even when that score leaves out the real scientific burden.

The concrete job is: Choose training terms that reward the behavior needed by the scientific claim. The evidence available is data errors, equation errors, boundary errors, weights between terms, constraints, and held-out checks. The hidden quantity is whether the trained model is good because the science is right or only because the written score is easy to lower. This is the first-principles chain: evidence is partial, the needed quantity is larger than the evidence, and the method is only a bridge between the two.

The bridge matters because someone will make a decision: decide whether the loss is a faithful contract for the claim being made. That decision gives the math its burden. Average visual similarity is not enough if the decision depends on a boundary layer, a rare event, a peak stress, a force, a concentration, or another local quantity. The domain test must therefore be: change the weights, hold out hard regions, and check the physical or decision quantity directly.

How The Methods Enter Without Jargon

Write each score term in plain language. Tie each term to a scientific requirement. Check whether lowering the score improves the real target quantity. Read these as jobs, not labels. One method names how the quantity is allowed to change. Another makes a fast stand-in. Another keeps geometry or field structure visible. Another asks where belief should weaken. The right method is the one that carries the missing information needed for the decision and exposes the first condition where it breaks.
